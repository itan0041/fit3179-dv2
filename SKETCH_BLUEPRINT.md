# A4 Sketch Blueprint — "Grow Where You Are"

**Draw this by hand, on paper, in pen.** The rubric gives the sketch **0 marks if it was made with digital tools**, and a **−3% penalty if the sketch is for a different topic than the final page**. This blueprint is generated from the finished `index.html`, section for section and figure for figure, so what you draw matches what you submit.

Use **one A4 sheet, portrait**. Pencil first, then go over in black pen. Keep it clean and tidy — the HD descriptor is "four or more clear sections, headings, text, figures, with detailed, creative and varied charts and maps."

The page has **six sections and fourteen figures**. Almost everything is full width: only **two** rows are split into a left and a right half (Figures 9 + 10, and Figures 12 + 13). Draw it that way — the marker compares the sketch to the page.

Also: the unit requires you to **acknowledge AI use** on the submission. We will draft that acknowledgement for you later.

---

## Overall page frame

Draw a **tall rectangle** on the A4 (leave ~1.5 cm margin all round). The page is **one scrolling column, 1180 px wide**, so sketch it as a narrow tall page split into **seven bands** — a title block and the six sections. Rule a light horizontal line between each band and write the band number in the left margin.

Every section opens the same way, so draw the opener the same way each time:

```
SECTION NUMBER · SHORT NAME      <- small, all caps
Big headline across the page     <- hand-lettered
one line of subtitle underneath  <- lighter
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~  |  ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~   <- body text as squiggles, TWO columns
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~  |  ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
```

Headline and subtitle run the **full width at the top**; the body text sits **below them in two columns**. Keep all panel left edges on the same vertical line — the marker is explicitly looking for **sight-line alignment**.

The upper one finishes **above** the start line, the lower one **below** it. **Shade the gap between them.** Label the upper line *land planted*, the lower *tonnes harvested*, and rule a faint horizontal line labelled *2014 = 100*. ## Band 3 — THREE · THE MAP

> This band and the next two carry the map requirement. **Three or more map idioms, or the Idioms mark is capped at half.** There are four. Label each with its idiom name.

Every map on this page is drawn as **two panels side by side**: Peninsular Malaysia (a lumpy vertical blob, smaller, on the **left**) and Sabah + Sarawak (a wider blob on the **right**). Draw it that way every time — same two shapes, same sizes. Don't fuss over accuracy.

- Headline: **Where Malaysia's vegetables actually grow**
- **Figure 5 (full width) — choropleth.** The two panels, states shaded in 4–5 densities of hatching. Graded legend bar below. Caption: *vegetable land per 1,000 residents, 2024*. Write **"choropleth"**.
- **Figure 6 (full width) — proportional symbol map.** The same two panels, with **circles of clearly different sizes** on each state — one very large on Pahang. Nested-circle legend. Caption: *total vegetable production, 2024*. Write **"proportional symbol map"**.
- Callout arrow onto the Selangor/KL area: *"most people, least growing"*.

## Band 4 — FOUR · THE MISMATCH

- Headline: **Searching for a garden, and having one, are different maps**
- **Figure 7 (full width) — bivariate choropleth.** The two panels shaded again, and **below them, on the left**, the signature **3×3 square colour key** with arrows labelled *"searches →"* and *"vegetable land ↑"*. Write a line of explanation to the right of the key. Write **"bivariate choropleth"**.

## Band 5 — FIVE · THE SHIFT

- Headline: **The growth went east. The losses went where the people are.**
- **Figure 8 (full width) — spike map.** The two panels again, with **vertical needles of different heights** rising out of each state, some up and some down, with `+156`, `+112`, `−55` written at a few spike tips. Write **"spike map"**.
- Now the **first split row**. Rule a gutter down the middle:
  - **Figure 9 (left half) — dumbbell chart.** Fourteen state names down the left, each with two dots joined by a short horizontal line. Some lines point right, some left. Small legend: *pale dot = 2014, coloured dot = 2024*. Write **"dumbbell"**.
  - **Figure 10 (right half) — waffle chart.** A **10×10 grid** of small squares. Shade the first ~36 darkest and label them *Pahang*; the rest in lighter shades down to a pale remainder in the bottom-right corner. The key runs **along the bottom**, not the side. Write **"waffle — share of national vegetable area"**.

## Band 6 — SIX · THE CALENDAR

- Headline: **No spring, no autumn — here, the rain sets the calendar**
- **Figure 11 (full width) — heatmap.** A grid, **12 columns (Jan→Dec) by 16 rows (states)**, month names along the **top**. Shade cells in varying density, darkest in a block at the right-hand end (Nov–Dec) and along the top row (Sarawak). Graded key below. Write **"heatmap — monthly rainfall by state"**.
- The **second split row**:
  - **Figure 12 (left half) — custom radial chart.** A circle divided into 12 wedges like a clock face, **Jan at the top**, months running clockwise. Two closed outlines on it — one roughly round, one lopsided. Legend: *Malaysia* vs *Worldwide*. Write **"radial chart — seasonality"**.
  - **Figure 13 (right half) — dual-axis chart.** Bars rising towards the **right** end (rainfall, left axis) with a line crossing them going the **other** way (search interest, right axis). Label both axes. Write **"dual axis"**. Annotate: *"wettest month, quietest searches"*.
- **Figure 14 (full width) — interval chart.** Sixteen state names down the left, a 12-month axis along the **top**, and one **horizontal bar per state** spanning its three driest months — the bars step down the page from a January start to a June start. Graded key below. Write **"interval chart — the three driest months"**.
